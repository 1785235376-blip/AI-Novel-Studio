## Serializer design

All serializers are pure functions. They do not open sessions, access databases, write models, or mutate model JSON values. `split_internal_fields()` copies its input, removes `_source_id` and `_source_order`, and filters reserved fields before extension data is merged.

Reserved fields are `id`, `name`, `age`, `status`, `privacy_level`, `facts`, and `details`. ORM-owned fields are applied after extension dictionaries, preventing JSONB content from corrupting identity or canonical fields.

## Field mapping

- Character: `slug → id`, `life_status → status`, expands `facts`, removes nested/internal fields, orders by `facts._source_order`.
- Location: `slug → id`, expands `facts`, orders by `facts._source_order`.
- Timeline: `event_time → time`, expands `details`, restores `details._source_id`, orders by `(sequence, _source_order)`, and emits privacy only when source details carry that field.
- Secret: restores UUID-to-business-ID mapping from `novels.metadata.context_source_ids.secrets`, orders by mapping order, and keeps public redaction separate from Context visibility.
- Foreshadowing: restores `details._source_id`, removes internal fields, and prioritizes `_source_order`.
- Canon: preserves `fact_value.id`, source, and privacy; UUID is only a fallback for historical rows without a business ID.

`PostgresNovelRepository.get_data_set()`, `get_public_secrets()`, `get_context_sources()`, and `_canon()` now use this shared boundary.
